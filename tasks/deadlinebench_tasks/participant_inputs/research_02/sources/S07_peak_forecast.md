# S07: Peak forecast and decision timing, 8 May 2026
SYNTHETIC: All forecasts and plans are invented.

[S07-P1] The next peak season begins in ten weeks. Central annual volume is 2.4 million parcels and the central oversize mix is 40%, as set in S01. During peak weekends oversize could be 50%; some shifts could include more new staff. The past baseline parcel mix was lower in oversize than the planning mix. This makes stratified projection useful, but it does not remove all workload differences.

[S07-P2] Facilities can inspect candidate scanner lanes within two weeks. Supervisor cover for a RouteCraft refresher test can be reserved for four weeks, after which staffing is less certain. An eight-week common-workload comparison would deliver a decision before peak season, although it would leave little time for a broad launch. A 12-week plan is within the optional planning limit but should explain the peak-season timing tradeoff.

[S07-P3] Deferring the entire matter indefinitely could leave the cooperative with current rework costs and peak pressure. Rapid expansion could lock in a flow bottleneck or a training workflow that staff cannot maintain. Neither risk is monetized beyond the central avoided-event convention. A bounded next step with a specific decision date can address these risks without inventing a loss estimate.
