# S04: Peak-flow and scanner observation note, 24 April 2026
SYNTHETIC: All shifts, supervisors, and observations are invented.

[S04-P1] The six observed peak shifts in each arm were selected from scheduled busy periods, not randomly sampled across all peaks. Overall medians use all parcel types in those shifts. The oversize 95th percentile measures elapsed packing-to-chute cycle time, including scan retries and queue waiting. It is not the same measure as active paid packing productivity. Both interventions' observed overall medians exceed 180 parcels/hour; only RouteCraft's observed oversize p95 is within ten minutes. No safety incident was confirmed during these observed shifts.

[S04-P2] SignalGate operators reported repeated reads on creased oversize labels. On one shift, a trained supervisor bypassed the scan confirmation for 40 minutes after queues grew. That bypass is logged, not a safety incident, and its effect on the final event rate is not separable in these aggregate counts. The operator representative prefers a mount repair before adding lanes. A vendor engineer believes retries are largely a mounting issue, but firmware and mounting have not been separately tested.

[S04-P3] RouteCraft supervisors used launch-period reminders that were more intensive than the planned fortnightly refreshers. Oversize handling often included a peer check. The measured flow did not show a productivity collapse, but it is unclear whether the same performance would persist with less experienced staff or fewer reminders. Peer-check time is included in the paid-hour denominator.

[S04-P4] The baseline window and trial windows differ, and peak demand was not identical. Thus comparing 192, 184, and 196 does not estimate causal productivity change. It does identify a current operational concern: the SignalGate configuration observed in the initial test misses the oversize cycle-time gate. A promise of future firmware must not be reported as an observed pass.
