# S03: Handling trial method note
SYNTHETIC: All trial observations are invented. Use row IDs in S03_handling_trial.csv when citing numbers.

[S03-P1] Logged handling minutes are active agent minutes for answered tickets, including work on reopens occurring within seven days. They exclude waiting on the customer, idle time, and escalations that were never answered in these queues. Divide logged handling minutes by answered tickets to obtain each language's mean. To project a different language mix, average the language means using that mix; do not pool each trial's own ticket counts and call that a 70/30 projection.

[S03-P2] Both vendor cohorts were volunteers with at least a year's tenure, but they were different people. Agents were not randomized between vendors. Each cohort had eight agents; the remaining two agents continued their usual work. Retrospective baseline queue filters attempted to match product line and priority. The trial windows coincided, while the baseline window was earlier. Ticket complexity was not labeled, and time-of-day distributions were not controlled.

[S03-P3] A product notice simplified one common billing question immediately before both vendor trials. That notice did not exist during baseline. Analysts cannot separate its contribution from the tools' contribution. Two highly experienced Spanish agents were in the CoveFind cohort and one in the EmberSearch cohort. This is relevant to comparison, but does not prove that expertise explains the observed difference.

[S03-P4] The logs support observed differences and planning projections. They do not establish a causal treatment effect, the size of an effect on novice agents, or statistical significance. No variance estimates are provided. Two weeks cannot establish long-run performance as policies change. Some tickets escalated outside the measured queues; escalation proportions were not retained consistently, so changes in the full workload remain unknown.
